Reading Washington's poverty data

These are the Census Bureau's official poverty estimates for all 39 Washington counties, 2014 to 2024. The data looks simple, but reading it well takes care: rates and counts tell different stories, small counties carry large uncertainty, and the official measure leaves out things that matter for policy.       About this data
      • Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates (SAIPE), state and county files for 2014–2024. SAIPE combines American Community Survey data with administrative records, which makes its estimates for small counties more reliable than survey data alone.
      • What "poverty" means here: the official poverty measure compares a family's pre-tax cash income to a national threshold that varies by family size. It does not count tax credits or noncash benefits like SNAP, and the threshold is the same everywhere in the country.
      • 90% ranges are the Census Bureau's 90% confidence intervals. When two counties' ranges overlap, the data can't reliably say which is higher.
      • Supplemental Poverty Measure figures in question 4 are national, from the Census Bureau working paper SEHSD-WP2022-24.
