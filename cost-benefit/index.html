Seawall or buyouts? How the discount rate decides

Shoreline, a low-lying neighborhood in the fictional Puget Sound city of Port Halden, floods more often each decade. Expected flood damage is about $5 million a year today and rising with the sea. The city is weighing two responses with very different timing. Change the assumptions and watch the recommendation move.

Seawall

  • Build: $40M a year for 3 years ($120M)
  • Upkeep: $1.5M a year once built
  • Must be raised in year 50: $60M
  • Prevents 90% of flood damage from year 3

Buyouts and wetland restoration

  • Buy 300 homes: $18M a year for 5 years ($90M)
  • Restore wetland in year 5: $10M
  • Lost property tax: $0.8M a year
  • Prevents damage gradually, reaching 70% by year 5, permanently
  • Optional: wetland benefits (habitat, recreation, carbon)

Assumptions

Federal guidance (OMB Circular A-4) set a 2% default in 2023. It was rescinded in February 2025, restoring the 2003 version's 3% and 7%.

Sea-level rise scenario

Non-market benefits are real but hard to price. Analysts often estimate them from surveys or comparable markets.

About this model
  • Fictional and simplified. Port Halden and every cost and benefit are invented for teaching. Real flood analyses model storm probabilities, uncertainty, and many more options.
  • How it works. Each year's benefits minus costs are divided by (1 + r)t, where r is the discount rate and t the year, then summed. Expected damage grows each year at the rate set by the sea-level rise scenario.
  • Discount rate guidance. OMB Circular A-4 (2023) set a 2% default. OMB memo M-25-15 (February 2025) rescinded it and reinstated the 2003 Circular, which uses 3% and 7%.
  • Left out on purpose, as questions for discussion: who bears the costs and who gets the benefits, what happens to the 300 displaced households, and the risk of a seawall failing in a storm bigger than it was built for.
  • Privacy. This page collects no data. It sets no cookies, uses no storage, and makes no network requests after it loads.
  • Accessibility. Every control works by keyboard. The present-value table gives the same results as the charts.
