Who gets a bus stop? Planning transit access north of the Ship Canal

Your team is planning a new frequent bus service between Ballard and the University District. You can fund 8 stops from the candidate locations on the map. Each stop serves the streets within about a 10-minute walk. Decide where to put them, then check who your plan reaches, and who it leaves out.

Draft for discussion with Prof. Zhao. Streets and walking areas are computed from OpenStreetMap; population comes from census tracts and is spread evenly along each tract's residential streets. Candidate stops, the budget, and the shocks are placeholders. Prof. Zhao's block-level data and pedestrian-network analysis will replace these approximations.

Discuss with your group

  1. Did your team aim to reach the most people, or the people who most depend on transit? How did that choice show up on the map?
  2. Which neighborhoods ended up farthest from any stop? Who lives there, and who might speak up for them in a real planning process?
  3. When the shock hit, what did you give up first, and why? Did your revised plan become more or less equitable?
About this draft and its data
  • Streets: © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License. Includes streets, stairways, and trails such as the Burke-Gilman; freeways are excluded. Streets south of the Ship Canal are left out.
  • Walking areas: all streets within 800 meters along the network (about 10 minutes at a typical walking pace) of each candidate stop, precomputed. Hills are not yet accounted for.
  • Population: City of Seattle, Racial and Social Equity Composite Index (2026 release, American Community Survey 5-year estimates), by census tract: total population, people of color, and people below 200% of the poverty level ("lower income" here). Each tract's residents are spread evenly along its residential streets; trails and stairways carry no residents. Tracts are included when their center lies in the study area.
  • Placeholders to confirm with Prof. Zhao: candidate stop locations, the 8-stop budget, the two shocks, walking distance and speed, and the move to block-level population.
  • Privacy. This page collects no data. A group's plan is saved only in the link it copies.
  • Accessibility. Stops can be chosen from the map or the list above, by keyboard or mouse. The scorecard states results in text.
