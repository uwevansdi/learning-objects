Design a bus line: who does your route serve?

Your team is designing a new frequent bus line north of the Ship Canal. A bus line isn't a scatter of stops: stations go in order, the bus has to drive the streets between them, and riders won't use a route that wanders. Pick your stations, check who lives within a 10-minute walk of them, and watch who your line leaves out.

    Discuss with your group

    1. Your route had to be direct and connected. Which neighborhoods did that rule out, even though you wanted to reach them? Is that a fair tradeoff for riders?
    2. Did your team aim for the most residents, or for the residents who most depend on transit? How did that choice change your route?
    3. When the shock hit, what did you cut first, and who lost access? Did your revised line become more or less equitable?
    About this draft and its data
    • Streets: © OpenStreetMap contributors, Open Database License. Walking uses streets, stairways, and trails; the bus uses streets only and prefers arterials. Freeways and areas south of the Ship Canal are excluded.
    • Walking areas: streets within 800 meters along the network (about 10 minutes on foot) of each station. Hills are not yet accounted for.
    • Bus paths: the shortest street path between two stations, with local streets counted as 1.6 times longer so the bus favors arterials. Travel time assumes an average of 18 km/h including stops.
    • Population: City of Seattle Racial and Social Equity Composite Index (2026 release, ACS 5-year estimates), by census tract: total population, people of color, and people below 200% of the poverty level ("lower income"). Residents are spread evenly along each tract's residential streets.
    • To confirm with Prof. Zhao: candidate stations, the route rules, travel speed, the shocks, and block-level population.
    • Privacy. This page collects no data. A group's plans are saved only in the link it copies.
    • Accessibility. Stations can be added, removed, and reordered from the lists above by keyboard. The rule checks and scorecard state results in text.
