About these estimates

What's counted. Citizen voting-age population: U.S. citizens age 18 and older, the group eligible to register and vote. It's not a count of registered voters or of people who voted.

Where it comes from. The U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS) five-year estimates, prepared for Washington's precincts and legislative districts by the Washington elections data project at UW CSDE. Each map year uses the five-year survey shown next to it (for example, 2010 uses 2008–2012).

These are estimates. They come from a survey sample, so every number has a margin of error, and it's largest for small places and small groups. Totals are rounded. Precincts with fewer than 50 eligible citizens are shaded with stripes and their percentages aren't shown. Treat those under 200 with caution.

Some years repeat. 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 all use the same survey (2020–2024), so differences between them come only from new precinct lines. The "change since" view won't compare years from the same survey, and it notes when two survey periods overlap.

Race and ethnicity. Hispanic or Latino people may be of any race. The other groups count people who are not Hispanic or Latino. "Two or more races" combines the multiracial categories in the source. People of color means everyone except non-Hispanic white people. The diversity index is the chance, out of 100, that two eligible citizens picked at random belong to different groups.

Precinct lines change. Counties redraw precincts often, so a precinct's numbers can change because its boundaries moved. Each year is drawn with that year's precinct map from the Washington Secretary of State. A few counties' files were incomplete (Pierce and Asotin in 2015, and a handful elsewhere), so those precincts use the same precinct's lines from the nearest year. 2011 uses the 2012 lines. "Change since" for precincts only compares precincts whose lines stayed about the same.

Legislative districts. The 49 districts have used three maps here: the 2012 map, the 2022 map, and the 2024 map, which a federal court redrew in the Yakima Valley under the Voting Rights Act. The district table for 2016–2019 uses the 2012 map, 2020–2021 the 2022 map, and 2022–2025 the 2024 map. District trends and changes are estimated by adding up precincts inside today's lines. They can differ slightly from the official district table.
