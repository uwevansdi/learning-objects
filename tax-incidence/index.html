Who really pays a tax on soda?

In 2018 Seattle began taxing sweetened drinks at 1.75¢ per ounce, or about $0.21 on a 12-oz drink. The law collects the tax from distributors. But who the law bills (statutory incidence) is not the same as who ends up worse off (economic incidence). Move the sliders to see how the burden splits between buyers and sellers, and how much activity the tax prevents.

About this model
  • Illustrative, not estimated. The market starts at $1.50 per drink and 10 million drinks per month. Those numbers, and the elasticities, are teaching values, not estimates of Seattle's market.
  • Linear curves. Both curves pass through the no-tax equilibrium, and the sliders set each curve's price elasticity at that point. With linear curves, buyers bear εS / (εS + εD) of the tax, and deadweight loss is ½ × tax × drop in quantity.
  • What it leaves out: cross-border shopping as a separate market, substitution to untaxed drinks, market power, and the health benefits that motivate the tax. Those are good questions for class discussion.
  • Privacy. This page collects no data. It sets no cookies, uses no storage, and makes no network requests after it loads.
  • Accessibility. Every control works by keyboard. The results table and the summary sentence give the same information as the graph.
