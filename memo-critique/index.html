Mark up the memo

A staff analyst wrote this memo to a city council member who has five minutes to read it before a vote. It has problems. Find them: click any sentence and tag what's wrong, then check what the memo leaves out. When you're done, compare your markup with an expert editor's and read a revised version.

What's missing? Check everything a council member would need that this memo doesn't give them.

About this exercise
  • Fictional. Riverton, its council, its transit pass, and every number in both memos are invented for teaching. The memo's flaws are planted on purpose.
  • The checklist reflects common guidance for policy memos: lead with the recommendation, separate what the evidence shows from what you hope it shows, state costs and alternatives, and write for a busy reader.
  • Privacy. Your markup stays in this browser tab. Nothing is saved or sent, and reloading clears it.
  • Accessibility. Each sentence is a button, so the memo can be marked up by keyboard: Tab to a sentence, press Enter, then choose a problem.
